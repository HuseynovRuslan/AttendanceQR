## What is the problem?

On a paper log, anyone can sign for a colleague. A simple QR code can also be photographed and scanned from somewhere else. As a result, managers do not see the real picture, and employees' work is calculated incorrectly.

QRLog solves this not with one check but with four layers. Each layer asks a different question.

## 1. QR signature

**Question: is the code genuine?**

The QR poster at your workplace is not just a picture. It contains a digital signature that the system verifies. If the code has been altered, forged or has expired, the check-in is rejected.

## 2. Device

**Question: is this the employee's phone?**

Each employee is linked to their own phone. An attempt to check in from someone else's account or from a different phone is recognised by the system.

## 3. Location

**Question: is the employee in the right place?**

At the moment of scanning, the phone's GPS data is checked. If the employee is not inside the defined area of the branch, the check-in is not completed. That is why photographing the QR poster and scanning it from home does not work.

> The goal is not to watch employees, but to make sure the work of honest people is counted correctly.

## 4. Face

**Question: is the person scanning really them?**

On check-in, the front camera takes a photo and it is automatically compared with the employee's reference face. If there is a mismatch, that row is flagged in the manager's panel.

An important privacy point: the check-in photo is visible only in such flagged rows. There is no separate photo gallery.

## Conclusion

When the four checks work together, the "let a friend scan for me" trick becomes much harder, and managers see real attendance at the end of the day.
